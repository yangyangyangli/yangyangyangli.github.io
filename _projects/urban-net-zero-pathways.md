  <div class="project-detail__content">
    <section>
      <h2>Project overview</h2>
      <p>This research presents a GIS-based urban building framework using open data, building archetypes, and simplified 3D city modelling to evaluate pathways toward urban net-zero energy buildings in Canada.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Research challenge</h2>
      <p>Urban net-zero planning requires building-stock information and spatial methods that connect current energy conditions with building-level and city-scale intervention pathways.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Data and methodology</h2>
      <ul class="project-methods">
        <li>GIS and open urban data</li>
        <li>Simplified 3D city modelling</li>
        <li>Building archetypes and urban building energy modelling</li>
        <li>Spatial scenario assessment</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Net-zero pathways</h2>
      <p>The evaluated pathways include energy-efficiency retrofit, electrification, and renewable-energy integration.</p>
      <figure class="project-figure">
        <img src="{{ '/assets/img/projects/urban-net-zero/richmond-energy-pathways.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Four Richmond maps showing high-EUI buildings, post-retrofit energy use, fossil-fuel heating, and electrification savings." width="1600" height="1139" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
      </figure>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Key spatial findings</h2>
      <p>The maps distinguish high-EUI buildings, post-retrofit energy use, fossil-fuel heating, and the spatial distribution of electrification savings.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>My contribution</h2>
      <p>Yang Li is the first author of the related peer-reviewed publication.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Implications for urban decision-making</h2>
      <p>The framework connects building-stock modelling with spatially explicit intervention scenarios for comparing urban retrofit, electrification, and renewable-energy pathways.</p>
    </section>
