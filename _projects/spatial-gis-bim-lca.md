  <div class="project-detail__content">
    <section>
      <h2>Project overview</h2>
      <p>This research integrates GIS, BIM, urban building energy modelling, and life-cycle assessment to spatially evaluate the whole-life environmental impacts of urban buildings and net-zero scenarios.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Research challenge</h2>
      <p>Operational-energy transitions can change impacts elsewhere in a building life cycle. Evaluating urban net-zero scenarios therefore requires a spatial framework that considers both operational and embodied processes.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Framework</h2>
      <ul class="project-methods">
        <li>GIS and BIM integration</li>
        <li>Urban building energy modelling</li>
        <li>Life-cycle and spatial environmental assessment</li>
        <li>Business-as-usual and urban-net-zero scenario comparison</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Life-cycle scope</h2>
      <p>The framework evaluates whole-life environmental impacts, including operational and embodied processes associated with urban buildings.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Spatial results</h2>
      <p>The results spatialize net-zero-energy potential and life-cycle environmental impacts across the urban building stock.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Scenario comparison</h2>
      <p>The urban net-zero energy building (UNZEB) scenario reduced total life-cycle emissions by approximately 40%, while highlighting burden shifting to upstream and downstream processes.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>My contribution</h2>
      <p>Yang Li is the first author of the related peer-reviewed publication.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Planning / policy significance</h2>
      <p>The framework makes the spatial distribution of whole-life impacts visible alongside energy scenarios, supporting assessment of both direct changes and potential burden shifting.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Related publication</h2>
