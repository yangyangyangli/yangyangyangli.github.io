  <div class="project-detail__content">
    <section>
      <h2>Project overview</h2>
      <p>This applied research developed a GIS-based urban building-stock model for city-scale sustainability assessment in Richmond.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Municipal context</h2>
      <p>The project was completed through the UBC Sustainability Scholars program with the City of Richmond as partner and BC Hydro as funder.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Data and methods</h2>
      <ul class="project-methods">
        <li>GIS, LiDAR, and building footprints</li>
        <li>3D building-stock modelling and building archetypes</li>
        <li>Urban building energy and GHG assessment</li>
        <li>Solar-energy potential</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>3D building-stock model</h2>
      <p>LiDAR and GIS inputs supported a 3D representation of Richmond’s building stock, with buildings classified by archetype.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Energy / emissions assessment</h2>
      <p>The official project record describes mapping building energy consumption and GHG emissions using georeferenced data and archetypes, alongside assessment of energy-efficiency measures and photovoltaic generation.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Decision-support relevance</h2>
      <p>The pilot considered energy-efficiency measures, photovoltaic energy generation, District Energy Utility expansion, and prioritization of rezoning applications for GHG-emission reduction.</p>
    </section>
