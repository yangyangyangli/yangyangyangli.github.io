    <section>
      <h2>Project overview</h2>
      <p>This report examines best practices for integrating low-carbon, climate-resilient features into rooftop systems on Part 3 buildings in the City of New Westminster.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Research challenge</h2>
      <p>The project considers how rooftop design and retrofit decisions can bring together low-carbon technologies and climate-resilience measures.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Methods / workflow</h2>
      <ul class="project-methods">
        <li>Solar photovoltaic and solar thermal systems</li>
        <li>Green roofs and cool roofs</li>
        <li>Climate resilience</li>
        <li>Retrofit decision support</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Results / outputs</h2>
      <p>The report provides best-practice guidance and a high-level checklist intended to support effective rooftop retrofits. No quantitative results are stated here.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>My contribution</h2>
      <p>Yang Li is the author listed on the official UBC Sustainability Scholars report.</p>
    </section>
    <section>
      <h2>Significance</h2>
      <p>The official project summary connects rooftop designs and retrofits with energy resilience and climate adaptation opportunities for the City of New Westminster.</p>
